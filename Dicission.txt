# Telecalling CRM: complete blueprint

This merges everything we discussed: the GitHub search, your pasted scaffold, the RMDialer findings, the call-capture and recording research, the Expo plus Kotlin decision, and the IVR plan. Where you hadn't decided something, I picked a default, and section 7 lists them.

## 0. Goal, scope, assumptions

**Goal:** Replace RMDialer with your own system. Telecallers call assigned leads from their own SIM and log a two-tap disposition and follow-up. Managers see live per-caller results. WhatsApp comes next, and cloud telephony/IVR later.

- **In v1:** Android callers, own-SIM calling through the system dialer, call-log capture, lead import and assignment, dispositions and follow-ups, live dashboard, device health, optional recording sync, WhatsApp templates.
- **Not in v1:** default-dialer role and incoming-call popups, iOS calling, in-app call-audio recording, IVR.
- **Assumptions (default):** 5 to 50 callers, one company (single tenant), India hosting, one TypeScript-first developer, APK handed out directly.

## 1. Hard constraints that shape the design

1. **Play Store:** The call-log permission group is limited to apps registered as the default Phone or Assistant handler. So the app ships as a signed APK, not through Play.
2. **Sideloading rules:** Developer verification started on 30 Sept 2026 in Brazil, Indonesia, Singapore and Thailand and expands globally in 2027. India is expected in the 2027 wave. Apps distributed outside Play register through the Android Developer Console. Google describes a limited account for up to 20 devices without ID or fee, aimed at hobbyists and students, so a business with 50 callers should plan on a full account. Register now.
3. **Recording:** Android blocked real call recording in version 6 and microphone recording in version 10, and Play bans the accessibility workaround. Only dialers that ship with the phone, or Google's, can access call audio. So our app never records audio. Callyzer's model is that the phone records and the app syncs the files. We use the same model, with cloud telephony as the later alternative.
4. **iOS:** It can't read call logs or place SIM calls this way. Callers use Android, and iPhone users use the web dashboard or cloud click-to-call.
5. **Background killing:** Xiaomi, Oppo, Vivo and others kill background apps, so the call log is the source of truth and everything is reconciled from it.
6. **Route A limit:** The call log shows duration and type, not why an unanswered call failed (busy, rejected, no answer). Only the default-dialer route (`InCallService`) exposes the disconnect cause. Accept this for v1.
7. **Over-the-air updates:** They publish JavaScript and assets only. Any Kotlin change needs a new APK on every phone.

**3.4 Rules and algorithms**

1. **Numbers:** Normalize to E.164 (default region IN) in both engine and API, and match only on normalized numbers.
2. **Dedupe key:** `hash(deviceId + callLogEntryId + startedAt + numberE164)`. The server enforces unique `(callerId, dedupeKey)`, so retries and reconciles can't duplicate, even after an app data clear.
3. **Outcome (SIM channel):** Outgoing with duration > 0 is connected. Outgoing with duration 0 is not connected (busy, no answer, and rejected can't be told apart in Route A). Incoming missed or rejected comes from the log type.
4. **Capture:** On call end the engine looks for the newest matching call-log entry (number, time window, SIM), retrying over several seconds because entries can appear late. If none turns up, reconcile catches it.
5. **Reconcile:** On app start, after each call, and in a periodic WorkManager job, read the call log since the last watermark, add missing entries to the outbox, and mark them "needs disposition".
6. **Sync:** Send outbox rows in batches with exponential backoff and jitter. The server answers per row (created, duplicate, rejected), and only acknowledged rows are marked synced.
7. **Queue order (server-computed):** Due follow-ups first, then new leads by priority and age. Skip leads in DND or outside the calling-hours window, and never offer one lead to two callers.
8. **Assignment:** Round-robin over active telecallers, optionally weighted. Transfers write assignment history.
9. **Recording match:** Scan the granted folder and match each file to a call by timestamp within a tolerance of call end. Filename patterns per phone brand come from server config. Unmatched files stay pending, never guessed.
10. **Config and versions:** Dispositions, stages, call gap, retry timing, and recorder patterns live on the server. The engine reports its version in the heartbeat, and updates are gated by it.

**3.5 Sequences**

*S1 Login and setup*
1. Screens call `/auth/login` and hand the tokens to `engine.setAuth`.
2. Screens register the device, fetch `/config`, and run the setup checklist (permissions, battery, recorder folder).
3. The engine starts heartbeat and reconcile.

*S2 Call and capture (main flow)*
1. Screens show the next lead from `/me/queue`, and the caller taps Call.
2. `engine.dial` uses the Telecom API to place the call on the chosen SIM, and the conversation happens in the phone's own call screen.
3. When the call ends, the call-state listener fires and the engine reads the call log, builds the dedupe key, writes to Room, and emits `callEnded`.
4. The disposition sheet opens, the caller picks disposition, remark, and follow-up, and `saveDisposition` updates Room.
5. WorkManager posts the batch to `POST /calls`, and the API upserts by dedupe key.
6. The API updates the lead, publishes to the live gateway, and queues a WhatsApp job if the disposition has a template. The dashboard updates over SSE.

*S3 Reconcile after the app is killed*
1. Android still logs the calls while the app is dead.
2. On the next start or periodic job, the engine adds missing calls as "needs disposition".
3. The caller completes the pending list, and sync proceeds as usual.
4. A heartbeat gap during shift hours flags the device on the dashboard.

*S4 Recording sync (optional)*
1. The phone's own dialer records the call (auto-record is enabled per phone).
2. The engine scans the granted folder and matches the file to a call.
3. It requests an upload URL, uploads directly to storage, and links the file with `POST /recordings`.
4. The web dashboard plays it through a short-lived URL.

*S5 WhatsApp follow-up*
1. A disposition with a template is saved.
2. The API checks consent and calling-hours rules and queues a job.
3. A worker sends the template and records a MessageLog entry.
4. Delivery webhooks update the status on the lead timeline.

*S6 Cloud call (later)*
1. A click-to-call request goes to `TelephonyProvider.clickToCall`.
2. The provider rings the caller's verified mobile, then the customer.
3. Its webhook reaches the API, which verifies it, queues it, and replies fast. The result lands in CallLog (channel CLOUD, unique providerCallId, recording link).
4. A push opens the same disposition sheet in the app.

**3.6 Security and privacy**

- **Tokens:** Use TLS everywhere. Access tokens are short-lived (default 15 min). Refresh tokens rotate, are hashed in the database, and are bound to a device, with remote revoke. The engine owns refresh, so the UI and engine can't invalidate each other's rotated tokens.
- **Access control:** Telecallers see only their own leads and calls, managers their team, and admins everything. The audit log covers assignment and config changes.
- **Call-log privacy:** The engine uploads only calls to or from numbers that exist as leads, or that it dialed itself. Everything else stays on the phone. Prefer company-owned phones, and disclose monitoring to staff.
- **Storage and webhooks:** Presigned URLs are short-lived (default 10 min) and recordings are private with a retention policy. Verify webhook signatures or tokens, and make processing idempotent. Rate-limit auth and webhooks, and keep secrets out of git.
- **Compliance:** Calling-hours and DND/consent checks run on server and engine. Tell customers calls are recorded. Have someone qualified review TRAI, DND, and DPDP rules before the pilot. I'm not a lawyer.

**3.7 Deployment, capacity, observability**

- **Environments:** dev (Docker Compose), staging, and production use the same images, with `prisma migrate deploy` in CI/CD.
- **Hosting:** API and web run as containers, with managed Postgres (backups and point-in-time recovery), Redis, and an S3-compatible bucket in an India region.
- **Releases:** CI builds the API and web, and builds and signs the APK for your download page. Over-the-air updates cover UI changes, and engine versions are tracked per device.
- **Capacity (assumption):** 50 callers at about 150 calls a day is roughly 7,500 calls a day, plus a heartbeat per phone per minute. One API instance and one Postgres are plenty at that size.
- **Monitoring:** Sentry and Crashlytics, heartbeat diagnostics (permissions, battery status, last call-log read, outbox size, last error, phone model), BullMQ failed-job alerts, and alerts for stale devices and webhook errors.

## 6. Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Capture unreliable on some phone brands | Phase 1 gate; reconcile; drop the brand or move those callers to cloud click-to-call |
| Background killing | Setup checklist, battery exemption, heartbeat alerts, identical company phones |
| Recording varies by phone | Standardize phones, per-phone guide, test your models; cloud telephony for must-record calls |
| Engine changes force APK reinstalls | Small mechanism-only engine, server-driven policy, versioned interface, in-app update check |
| Sideloading friction | Register as a developer now; install guide; device management later |
| Legal and privacy (TRAI, DND, DPDP, recording consent) | Consent, DND, and calling-hours built in; upload only lead-matched calls; legal review before pilot |
| Expo SDK upgrades every few months | Budget a day or two per upgrade; pin versions; keep the engine independent |
| Scope creep (default dialer, IVR, iOS) | Parked as "later"; the channel abstraction keeps the door open |

## 7. Decisions made and open items

**Decided in our conversation**
1. Build your own system, and keep RMDialer running until the pilot passes. RMDialer is a paid closed-source app. Its GitHub org only holds API and sample material.
2. Own-SIM calling, Android only.
3. Route A (system dialer plus call log) for v1; default-dialer role later.
4. Expo screens plus a Kotlin engine (default). Compose screens are a drop-in alternative with the engine unchanged.
5. Direct APK distribution, with developer registration.
6. Recording through phone-recorder sync (optional), with cloud telephony later for must-record calls.
7. IVR later through the channel abstraction in the schema.
8. Official WhatsApp API, with n8n optional.
9. Compliance built in, with legal review before the pilot.
10. Small, versioned engine; policy lives on the server.

# Final plan

**What we're building:** Your own telecalling system that replaces RMDialer. Callers dial leads from their own SIM, work fully offline, and everything syncs when the phone reconnects. Managers see live results on a web dashboard. WhatsApp follow-ups come next, and cloud telephony/IVR later.

**Final decisions**
1. Build your own system, and keep RMDialer running until the pilot passes.
2. Android only. Calls go through the phone's own dialer, and the call log supplies exact duration and outcome. The default-dialer role comes later, only if needed.
3. Expo screens on top of a small Kotlin engine. Compose screens are a drop-in alternative, and the engine stays unchanged.
4. Offline-first: the phone holds the working copy and the server settles conflicts.
5. Hand the signed APK directly to callers.
6. Recording is optional: phones record, and the app syncs the files. Cloud telephony covers calls that must be recorded.
7. Official WhatsApp Business API, with n8n optional.
8. Compliance is built in, and someone qualified reviews it before the pilot.
9. The engine stays small and versioned, and rules live on the server.

**Constraints behind the design**
- Google Play only allows the call-log permission group for apps registered as the default Phone or Assistant handler, so the app ships as an APK, not through Play.
- Google's developer verification started on 30 Sept 2026 in four countries and expands globally in 2027. India is expected in the 2027 wave, and apps outside Play register through the Android Developer Console. The limited account for up to 20 devices is aimed at hobbyists and students, so plan on a full account for a team of 50.
- Android 6 blocked real call recording and Android 10 blocked microphone recording, and only dialers that ship with the phone or come from Google can access call audio. So our app never records audio. Callyzer's approach is the same as ours: the phone records and the app syncs the files.
- iOS can't do this, so iPhone users use the web dashboard. Some phone brands kill background apps, so the call log is the source of truth and everything reconciles from it.

## How offline and online sync works

- **Pull:** `GET /sync/pull?cursor=…` returns changed leads, calls, follow-ups, and config, plus deletions. The cursor is a server change number, not a timestamp.
- **Push:** `POST /sync/push` sends ordered mutations with client-generated IDs. The server answers per mutation: applied, duplicate, conflict, or rejected. Retries are always safe.
- **Outbox:** The engine stores generic mutations (call captured, disposition saved, follow-up done, lead edited). Screens enqueue the mutation first, then update the UI, and re-apply pending mutations over pulled data.
- **Conflicts:**
  - Calls are append-only.
  - Dispositions are versioned, and a mismatch goes to a "needs attention" list.
  - Managers' assignment and stage changes win, and caller-editable lead fields merge field by field.
  - Follow-ups use last write wins, and "done" never reverts.
  - One active device per caller.
- **Offline limits (defaults):** Screens lock after 72 hours without a sync, while call capture continues. WhatsApp templates older than 6 hours go to a manager instead of sending late.
- **Clocks:** The server stores each phone's clock offset to correct call times.
